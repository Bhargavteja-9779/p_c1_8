Dear Editors,
We submit the manuscript entitled “What can a pilot set tell about a calibration learning curve?
An observability limit for sample-size planning in spectroscopy” for consideration as a Research
Paper in Analytica Chimica Acta.
Every multivariate calibration project faces the same early decision: will more reference analyses
reduce the prediction error enough to justify their cost? Learning-curve extrapolation from a pilot
set has been the standard answer since Ali et al. (Anal. Chem. 2018). Our manuscript shows, with
a proof and a leakage-free benchmark on 38 public NIR/MIR calibration tasks, which part of a
learning curve a pilot can determine and which part it cannot.
The methodological contributions are: (i) Proposition 1, which locates the observability horizon
of a pilot set and shows that the gain from more samples is not identifiable without prior
information; (ii) a computable, prior-free compatible set and an information fraction that tell the
analyst, for every pilot, whether the sample-size question can be settled; and (iii) Bayesian
regularisation-path matching (B-RPM), a forecaster that combines a transferable structural prior
with the task's unlabelled spectra and the pilot's exact cross-validation path. B-RPM predicted
gains better than the no-improvement rule and, unlike a transferred empirical slope, kept its
accuracy on the soil tasks when its prior came from other application families. We report openly
that the pilot's own cross-validated error remained the best estimate of the RMSEP level and that
three of four pre-specified hypotheses were not confirmed. The positive practical result is a
campaign-planning case study. With its prior taken from other application families, decisioncalibrated B-RPM came close to the requested 80% and 90% success rates (72% and 88%),
whereas a transferred empirical slope delivered 28% and 40%.
The work connects to current themes in Analytica Chimica Acta: validation design and leakage,
the error structure of reference values and replicates, and calibration-set size. The study follows a

time-stamped, pre-specified design. The new method and its hypotheses were fixed before the
results were seen, negative outcomes are reported, all data are public, and the complete code
(one-command rerun) will be made available on request.
The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors
approved the submission and declare no competing interests.
